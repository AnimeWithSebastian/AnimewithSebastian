PERSONAL_RATING FORMAT SPEC (added June 15, 2026):
  When to use: Creator has recently watched a show and has a genuine take on it. Works for any show — airing, finished, or classic.
  Goal: Creator gives a real rating with a real reason. Not a review. Not a recap. A verdict with a specific argument behind it.
  Structure:
    Hook (5-10 sec): The rating up front — don't bury it. Start with the number or the verdict.
    The argument (20-30 sec): ONE specific reason this rating is right. Not a list of pros and cons. One thing.
    The honest take (10-15 sec): What it does that most shows don't — or what it fails at that kills it.
    CTA: Ask people to rate it themselves. "What would you give it and why" drives comments.
  Rules:
    - This is a PERSONAL take. First person. "I think", "for me", "the way I see it."
    - Do NOT hedge. A 7/10 with no conviction is dead content. A 7/10 with a sharp argument is a debate.
    - The rating itself is not the content. The REASON for the rating is.
    - No spoilers in the VO. The argument should work for someone who hasn't watched it yet.
    - Fact Verification still applies to any specific claims made.
    - Clip Law #73 applies. Confirm clips exist before writing them into the edit order.
    - This format does NOT require SEARCH 2-4 (show traction/gap research). Go straight to SEARCH 5 after picking the show.
    - Show selection: pull from creator's recent watch history if known, otherwise pick from Tier 1 or Tier 2 Priority Shows.

RECOMMENDATION FORMAT SPEC (added June 15, 2026):
  When to use: A show or manga that not enough people are talking about — or a new season/manga that just dropped and deserves attention.
  Goal: Make someone feel like they discovered something. Not a trailer. Not hype. A specific reason this is worth their time.
  Structure:
    Hook (5-10 sec): Lead with the one thing about this show/manga that most people don't know. Surprising detail, creator backstory, or overlooked angle.
    The pitch (15-20 sec): What it actually is — genre, feel, comparable — in 2 sentences max. No plot recap.
    Why now (10-15 sec): Why this specific moment is the right time to start — new season, recent buzz, or something that just changed in the story.
    CTA: "Have you read/watched this" — yes/no debate with a follow-up: "if not, what are you waiting for."
  Rules:
    - MANGA ELIGIBLE: This format applies equally to manga recommendations, not just anime.
    - Never recap the plot. The pitch is a feeling and a reason, not a summary.
    - "People should watch this" is not a hook. "This manga was rejected 17 times before it became [X]" is.
    - The recommendation must feel personal — something the creator actually believes in, not just what's trending.
    - Fact Verification applies to all specific claims (creator backstory, sales numbers, episode counts, etc.).
    - Clip Law #73 applies. For manga recommendations: panels can be shown — confirm they exist and note chapter/volume.
    - This format works as both Short AND long-form intro. If the show is rich enough, flag it as long-form eligible.

NEW_ANIME_INTRO FORMAT SPEC (added June 15, 2026):
  When to use: Show is in its FIRST 2 WEEKS of airing. No prior Anime With Sebastian content on this show.
  Goal: Tell someone who hasn't watched it yet why they should — but from a specific angle, not a recap.
  Structure:
    Hook (5-10 sec): One specific thing about the show that most people don't know yet.
    Setup (15-20 sec): What the show actually is — in 2-3 sentences, no spoilers.
    The angle (20-30 sec): The one thing that makes this show different from everything else airing.
    CTA: A question that only someone who's already watching can answer.
  Rules:
    - NO plot recap. Never summarize the story.
    - Lead with something surprising — a production detail, a real-world origin, a creator backstory.
    - The viewer should finish the video thinking "I need to watch this" OR "I already watch this and I never knew that."
    - FACT VERIFICATION BLOCK still required. Claims about the show must be verified.
    - Clip Law #73 applies. All clip references must be verified or flagged UNVERIFIED.

VILLAIN_DEFENSE FORMAT SPEC (added June 15, 2026):
  When to use: A character the fandom calls a villain — but you think they were right, understandable, or simply misread.
  Goal: Make the viewer reconsider someone they already wrote off. Not a full character study — just one sharp argument.
  Structure:
    Hook (5-10 sec): State the defense immediately. "[Character] wasn't the villain. [One-line argument]."
    The case (20-30 sec): Two or three specific moments that support your read. Manga/episode verified.
    Flip (10-15 sec): Acknowledge the counterargument in one sentence — then dismiss it or flip it.
    CTA: "Who's the real villain in [show]? Let me know."
  Rules:
    - Must take an actual stance. No "both sides" cop-out.
    - Clip and panel references must be verified or flagged UNVERIFIED per Law #73.
    - FACT VERIFICATION BLOCK required.

REWATCH_HIT FORMAT SPEC (added June 15, 2026):
  When to use: A show or arc that hits completely differently the second time — because of a detail, a callback, or a piece of foreshadowing you missed.
  Goal: Give a rewatch reason. Make the viewer want to go back right now.
  Structure:
    Hook (5-10 sec): "If you rewatched [show] you already know this." Or: "The first time through, you missed this."
    The detail (20-25 sec): One specific thing — a line, a visual, a name — that lands differently once you know the ending.
    Why it matters (10-15 sec): What it says about the writing, the character, or the story structure.
    CTA: "Did you catch this your first time?"
  Rules:
    - The detail must be real and verifiable. No invented foreshadowing.
    - FACT VERIFICATION BLOCK required. Law #73 applies.

ANIME_VS_MANGA FORMAT SPEC (added June 15, 2026):
  When to use: A show where the manga and the anime tell the same story differently — different pacing, added scenes, cut scenes, art direction changes, or ending differences.
  Goal: Give people a real reason to pick up the source material, OR defend the adaptation.
  Structure:
    Hook (5-10 sec): The specific difference — not "the manga is better," but a concrete thing that changed.
    The comparison (20-30 sec): What the manga did vs. what the anime did. Be specific about chapter/episode.
    Your read (10 sec): Which version works better for this particular moment — and why.
    CTA: "Manga reader or anime only? Let me know where you're at."
  Rules:
    - Must cite the specific chapter or episode for every comparison.
    - No universal "manga is always better" take — evaluate the specific moment.
    - FACT VERIFICATION BLOCK required. Law #73 applies.

WRONG_TAKE FORMAT SPEC (added June 15, 2026):
  When to use: A popular take in the anime community that is wrong, overrated, or based on a bad read of the source material.
  Goal: Correct the record with a specific argument. Not to be contrarian — to actually be right.
  Structure:
    Hook (5-10 sec): State the wrong take plainly. "The [fandom] has been saying [take] for years. They're wrong."
    Why it's wrong (20-25 sec): Two or three specific reasons — scenes, chapters, creator interviews. Verified only.
    The right read (10-15 sec): What the show actually says about this moment or character.
    CTA: "Come fight me in the comments."
  Rules:
    - The take being challenged must actually be widespread — don't invent a strawman.
    - Evidence must be verifiable. Law #52 and Law #73 apply.
    - FACT VERIFICATION BLOCK required.

FIRST_EPISODE_TEST FORMAT SPEC (added June 15, 2026):
  When to use: Any show — airing, classic, or new. Evaluate the first episode only as a hook mechanism.
  Goal: Tell the viewer whether this show earns you by episode 1 — and exactly what moment does it.

    Hook (5-10 sec): "[Show] has [X] minutes to get you. Here's the exact moment it either wins or loses you."
    The scene (20-25 sec): Describe the specific first-episode moment that works (or doesn't). Episode 1 only.
    The verdict (10-15 sec): Pass or fail — and why that moment works as a hook.
    CTA: "Did episode 1 get you?"
  Rules:
    - Episode 1 content ONLY. No plot from later episodes.
    - The moment cited must be verified — episode timestamp or scene description from source.
    - FACT VERIFICATION BLOCK required.

CHARACTER_COURT FORMAT SPEC (added June 15, 2026):
  When to use: A character decision that split the fandom — sacrifice, betrayal, power-up refusal, revenge choice, anything with a "why did they do that" argument.
  Goal: Put the decision on trial. Prosecution and defense. You deliver the verdict.
  Structure:
    Hook (5-10 sec): The decision. "[Character] chose [action]. Was that right?"
    Prosecution (15 sec): The strongest argument against the choice.
    Defense (15 sec): The strongest argument for the choice.
    Verdict (10 sec): Your actual call — and one reason.
    CTA: "What's your verdict?"
  Rules:
    - Both sides must be real arguments, not strawmen.
    - The decision must be a real canon moment — verified per Law #73.
    - FACT VERIFICATION BLOCK required.

SLEPT_ON FORMAT SPEC (added June 15, 2026):
  When to use: A show, arc, character, or moment that the mainstream anime community has ignored or underrated — and you have a specific reason it deserves more.
  Goal: Shine a light on something real. Not hype — a genuine argument for why it hits.

    Why it's slept on (10-15 sec): One honest reason it got overlooked — release window, bad marketing, weak opening episode.

  Rules:
