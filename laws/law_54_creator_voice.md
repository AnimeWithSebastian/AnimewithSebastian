## THE STANDARD

The gold standard voice (creator's own words, June 8 2026):
"hey do you know Sasuke was inspired by another short brooding dark powered
anime character..."

That is the tone. Casual. Direct. Drops you in like you're already in the
middle of a conversation with someone who watches the same stuff you do.
Not a presentation. Not a script being read. A person talking.

## THE THREE RULES

RULE 1 — STATE A POSITION IN THE FIRST 5 SECONDS
The VO must open with a specific claim, not a setup.
Wrong: "Sasuke is one of the most iconic characters in anime history..."
Right: "Sasuke wasn't original. Kishimoto built him from someone else."

RULE 2 — NO HEDGING LANGUAGE — EVER
Banned phrases in every VO line:
  - "arguably"
  - "some people think"
  - "kind of"
  - "in a way"
  - "you could say"
  - "it's possible that"
  - "many fans believe"
These make the script sound like an essay. The creator has opinions.
State them like you mean them.

RULE 3 — THE CLOSER MUST FEEL LIKE A REAL PERSON
The final line cannot be a wrapped-up conclusion or a summary of what was said.
It must feel like the creator landing on a thought in real time —
not handing in an assignment.
Wrong: "That's why Sasuke remains one of anime's most complex characters."
Right: "and nobody ever told you Kishimoto was looking at Hiei when he drew him."

## THE CONVERSATION TEST

After the VO draft is complete, read it out loud in one take.
Ask: does this sound like someone who actually watched this show
     talking to someone who also watches anime?

If any line sounds like it was written to be read — rewrite it.
If any line could have been written by someone who never watched the show — cut it.
If the closer sounds like a conclusion to a school essay — rewrite it.

## WHAT THIS LAW DOES NOT DO

This law does not change the facts. Law #52 and Law #58 still require
every claim to be verified. This law governs HOW the verified facts are delivered.

The facts are the same. The voice is yours.
