# LAW #67 — LONG-FORM CREATOR VOICE
**System:** AnimeWithSebastian — v5.2
**Added:** June 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Source:** Law #54 (Shorts) adapted for long-form format
**Applies to:** All long-form videos (5–8 minutes)

---

## THE LAW

The voice in long-form is the same voice as the Shorts.
Same person. Same tone. Same conversational register.

The only difference: long-form has more time.
More time does not mean more formal. It means more of the same person.

A viewer who found the channel through a Short and clicks a long-form video
expects to hear the same creator. If the long-form sounds like a different person —
more polished, more scripted, more structured — they leave.
The voice is the reason they clicked. Keep it.

---

## THE THREE RULES (same as Law #54, scaled for long-form)

### RULE 1 — STATE A POSITION IN THE FIRST 30 SECONDS
Shorts get 5 seconds. Long-form gets 30 — but not more.
Do not spend the first minute explaining what the video is about.
Do not intro yourself. Do not recap the show's plot.
Drop directly into the claim that made you make the video.

Wrong: "Today we're going to be talking about why Guts from Berserk is one of the most
tragic characters in all of anime, and I want to break down exactly why that is."
Right: "Guts never actually gets to grieve. Every time he gets close — something takes it
from him. That's been sitting with me for a while."

### RULE 2 — NO HEDGING LANGUAGE — EVER
Same ban as Law #54. No exceptions for long-form.
Banned: "arguably," "some people think," "kind of," "in a way," "you could say,"
"it's possible that," "many fans believe."

Long-form does not make hedging more acceptable. It makes it more visible.
A 7-minute video full of hedging sounds like a Wikipedia article being read aloud.
State the take. Own it. Let the viewer push back.

### RULE 3 — THE CLOSER MUST FEEL LIKE A REAL PERSON
Same rule as Law #54 — but harder in long-form because more time means
more temptation to wrap it up cleanly.

The long-form closer is not a summary. It is not "so as we've seen."
It is the creator landing on one final thought that still has something unresolved in it.

Wrong: "So that's why Guts remains one of the most compelling characters in anime history.
His journey is a testament to the resilience of the human spirit."
Right: "He keeps going. That's the whole thing. I don't know if that's strength
or just what's left when everything else is gone."

## LONG-FORM SPECIFIC ADDITIONS

### THE MID-VIDEO VOICE CHECK
In a Short, there is no mid-video. In long-form, there is.

Every 2 minutes of long-form, the voice must re-anchor.
The viewer should be able to feel the creator's presence throughout —
not just in the hook and closer.

Signs the voice has drifted mid-video:
- Sentences get longer and more formal
- Claims stop being personal and become observational
- The creator stops saying "I" and starts saying "we" or "one"
- The pacing slows into paragraph-reading mode

If any of these appear mid-video → rewrite that section.

### THE PERSONALITY SIGNATURE — ONCE PER 2 MINUTES
Law #54 requires one personality signature per Short.
Long-form requires one per every 2 minutes of content.

A personality signature is a line specific enough that another channel
could not copy-paste it. It reveals how the creator thinks, not just what.

Example: "I watched the Eclipse scene four times. Every time I focused on the wrong thing."
Only someone who rewatched obsessively and caught where their attention went would write that.

### THE CONVERSATION TEST (same as Law #54)
Read the full script aloud in one take.
Ask: does this sound like someone who actually watched this show
     talking to someone who also watches anime?
If any section sounds like a presentation → rewrite it.
If the energy drops for more than 30 seconds → restructure that section.

---

## AUTO-FAIL CHECK (long-form version)

Before publishing:
[ ] Does the video open with a specific claim within 30 seconds?
[ ] Is hedging language absent throughout the entire video?
[ ] Does the closer feel like a real person landing on a thought — not a conclusion?
[ ] Does the voice stay consistent throughout — or does it drift formal mid-video?
[ ] Is there at least one personality signature per 2 minutes of content?
[ ] Would a viewer who came from a Short feel like they found the same person?
