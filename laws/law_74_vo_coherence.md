# Law #74 — VO Hook-to-Body Coherence
**Added:** June 16, 2026
**Triggered by:** Ghost in the Shell morning VO — hook said "you need one detail," body delivered four unrelated details.

---

## The Law

**The hook makes a contract. The body must honor it exactly.**

If the hook says "one detail" — there is ONE detail in the VO. Not two. Not four with transitions between them. One.

If the hook says "here's what nobody talks about" — the body delivers one specific overlooked thing, not a general overview of the show.

If the hook says "this changes everything" — the body identifies the exact thing that changes, and explains how. Not a list of interesting facts.

The hook and the body must be the same sentence, two different ways. If you can remove the hook and the body still makes sense as a standalone paragraph, the hook was fake. Rewrite the hook or cut the body down until they match.

---

## The Failure Pattern

A VO fails this law when:

1. **The hook promises singularity and the body delivers plurality.**
   - Hook: "you need one detail before you watch this"
   - Body: premiere date + setting year + squad name + villain name
   - This is a bullet list. Not a VO.

2. **The hook promises specificity and the body delivers summary.**
   - Hook: "here's the thing nobody caught"
   - Body: general show overview with "and it's really interesting"
   - If there's no specific thing, there's no VO. Find the specific thing or pick a different angle.

3. **The body answers a different question than the hook asked.**
   - Hook: "why does this moment matter?"
   - Body: description of what happened in the moment, no explanation of why
   - Description is not analysis. The hook promised analysis.

---

## The Fix

Before finalizing any VO draft:

**Read the hook. Write down in one sentence what the hook promises.**
Then read the body. Write down in one sentence what the body actually delivers.

If those two sentences are not the same thing — rewrite until they are.

---

## The User's Standard

The user rewrites every VO before filming. The job is not to write the final VO. The job is to give the user a draft that is structurally coherent — a real argument with a real thread — so their rewrite is about voice and word choice, not about fixing a broken structure.

A VO that promises one detail and delivers four is not a rough draft. It is a failed draft. The user should never receive a failed draft.

---

## Examples

**FAILING VO (do not send):**
> "Before you watch this show, you need one detail. It premieres July 7. It's set in 2029. The main character leads a squad called the Shell Squad. And the villain is already named."

Hook promised one detail. Body delivered four. Structure is broken. Do not send.

**PASSING VO (send):**
> "The new Ghost in the Shell premieres July 7. And they already named the villain. The official premise lists the Puppet Master — the same entity from the 1995 film — as the first major threat Motoko and Section 9 are up against. That's not a coincidence or a callback. That's the whole setup. If you know what the Puppet Master actually is, you already know why that's a big deal."

One detail (Puppet Master named in official premise). Hook and body match. Structure holds.

---

## Applies To
- All VO drafts across all formats and slots
- Long-form and Shorts equally
- Any hook language that implies singularity, specificity, or a direct answer to a question
