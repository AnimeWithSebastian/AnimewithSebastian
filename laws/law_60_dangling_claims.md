# LAW #60 — DANGLING CLAIMS LAW
**System:** AnimeWithSebastian — v5.1
**Added:** June 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE

---

## THE LAW

Every claim opened in the VO must be expanded and closed in the same VO.

No name-dropping without follow-through. No teasing a fact without delivering the fact.

---

## WHAT TRIGGERED THIS LAW

Real viewer feedback on a published video:

> "you said Darling in the Franxx did something the fans never forgave it for... the process to not tell us what said thing was. just feels like you're reading off headings and not expanding on it."

That comment is the full definition of this law. A claim was opened. It was never paid off. The viewer was left holding a question that the VO never answered.

---

## THE CORE RULE

**If you open a claim, you close it in the same VO.**

That means:
- If you say a show "did something controversial" → you say what it did
- If you say a character "made a decision fans never forgave" → you say what the decision was
- If you say a fight scene "changed what anime could do" → you say how
- If you say a moment "broke the fandom in half" → you say what the moment was

---

## HARD EXAMPLES

### VIOLATION
> "Darling in the Franxx did something in its final arc that the fanbase never forgave it for."

This is a dangling claim. The viewer now has a question and no answer. The VO is done. They never got the information.

### FIXED
> "Darling in the Franxx spent its whole run on this one relationship — and then the final arc threw it out for a giant alien war nobody asked for. The fanbase never forgave that pivot."

Claim opened → claim explained → no dangling end.

---

### VIOLATION
> "Hiei had one of the most underrated power-ups in 90s anime."

Dangling. What power-up? Why underrated?

### FIXED
> "Hiei's Jaganshi transformation gave him a third eye with mind-reading and curse abilities — and most people just remember him for the dragon. That power-up is slept on."

Claim opened → content delivered → closed.

---

## THE TEASER EXCEPTION

A VO can tease a claim in CUT 1 if it delivers the answer before CUT 7.

This is called a setup-payoff loop. It is allowed. The payoff must land in the same VO.

What is NOT allowed: teasing without paying off. If CUT 1 opens a question and the VO ends without answering it → HARD STOP.

---

## THE HEADING TEST

Read each sentence and ask: "Does this sound like a heading on a list?"

- "Mob Psycho has a moment that redefines what it means to be powerful." → heading
- "In Mob Psycho, there's a scene where Mob finally lets go — and he doesn't explode into some big display of power. He just cries. That's the whole moment." → content

Headings are not content. If the VO is reading like a list of headings with no expansion → rewrite.

---

## ENFORCEMENT

**Pre-send check (Law #58):** Before any VO is sent, it is reviewed against Law #60.

Reviewer scans every factual or evaluative claim:
1. Is there a claim that promises information but doesn't deliver it? → HARD STOP
2. Is there a tease in an early cut with no payoff before CUT 7? → HARD STOP
3. Does any sentence read like a heading with nothing underneath it? → HARD STOP

If any dangling claim is found → expand it or remove it. Then re-check.
