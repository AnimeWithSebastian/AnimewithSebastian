# LAW #67 — LONG-FORM CREATOR VOICE
**System:** AnimeWithSebastian — v5.2
**Added:** June 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Source:** Law #54 (Shorts) adapted for long-form format
**Applies to:** All long-form videos (5–8 minutes)

---

## THE LAW

The voice in long-form is the same voice as the Shorts.
Same person. Same tone. Same conversational register.

The only difference: long-form has more time.
More time does not mean more formal. It means more of the same person.

A viewer who found the channel through a Short and clicks a long-form video
expects to hear the same creator. If the long-form sounds like a different person —
more polished, more scripted, more structured — they leave.
The voice is the reason they clicked. Keep it.

---

## THE THREE RULES (same as Law #54, scaled for long-form)

### RULE 1 — STATE A POSITION IN THE FIRST 30 SECONDS
Shorts get 5 seconds. Long-form gets 30 — but not more.
Do not spend the first minute explaining what the video is about.
Do not intro yourself. Do not recap the show's plot.
Drop directly into the claim that made you make the video.

Wrong: "Today we're going to be talking about why Guts from Berserk is one of the most
tragic characters in all of anime, and I want to break down exactly why that is."
Right: "Guts never actually gets to grieve. Every time he gets close — something takes it
from him. That's been sitting with me for a while."

### RULE 2 — NO HEDGING LANGUAGE — EVER
Same ban as Law #54. No exceptions for long-form.
Banned: "arguably," "some people think," "kind of," "in a way," "you could say,"
"it's possible that," "many fans believe."

Long-form does not make hedging more acceptable. It makes it more visible.
A 7-minute video full of hedging sounds like a Wikipedia article being read aloud.
State the take. Own it. Let the viewer push back.

### RULE 3 — THE CLOSER MUST FEEL LIKE A REAL PERSON
Same rule as Law #54 — but harder in long-form because more time means
more temptation to wrap it up cleanly.

The long-form closer is not a summary. It is not "so as we've seen."
It is the creator landing on one final thought that still has something unresolved in it.

Wrong: "So that's why Guts remains one of the most compelling characters in anime history.
His journey is a testament to the resilience of the human spirit."
Right: "He keeps going. That's the whole thing. I don't know if that's strength
or just what's left when everything else is gone."

---

## LONG-FORM SPECIFIC ADDITIONS

### THE MID-VIDEO VOICE CHECK
In a Short, there is no mid-video. In long-form, there is.

Every 2 minutes of long-form, the voice must re-anchor.
The viewer should be able to feel the creator's presence throughout —
not just in the hook and closer.

Signs the voice has drifted mid-video:
- Sentences get longer and more formal
- Claims stop being personal and become observational
- The creator stops saying "I" and starts saying "we" or "one"
- The pacing slows into paragraph-reading mode

If any of these appear mid-video → rewrite that section.

### THE PERSONALITY SIGNATURE — ONCE PER 2 MINUTES
Law #54 requires one personality signature per Short.
Long-form requires one per every 2 minutes of content.

A personality signature is a line specific enough that another channel
could not copy-paste it. It reveals how the creator thinks, not just what.

Example: "I watched the Eclipse scene four times. Every time I focused on the wrong thing."
Only someone who rewatched obsessively and caught where their attention went would write that.

### THE CONVERSATION TEST (same as Law #54)
Read the full script aloud in one take.
Ask: does this sound like someone who actually watched this show
     talking to someone who also watches anime?
If any section sounds like a presentation → rewrite it.
If the energy drops for more than 30 seconds → restructure that section.

---

## AUTO-FAIL CHECK (long-form version)

Before publishing:
[ ] Does the video open with a specific claim within 30 seconds?
[ ] Is hedging language absent throughout the entire video?
[ ] Does the closer feel like a real person landing on a thought — not a conclusion?
[ ] Does the voice stay consistent throughout — or does it drift formal mid-video?
[ ] Is there at least one personality signature per 2 minutes of content?
[ ] Would a viewer who came from a Short feel like they found the same person?

# LAW #68 — LONG-FORM COMMENT ENGINEERING
**System:** AnimeWithSebastian — v5.2
**Added:** June 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Source:** Law #55 (Shorts) adapted for long-form format
**Applies to:** All long-form videos (5–8 minutes)

---

## THE LAW

Long-form comments are deeper than Short comments.
A viewer who watched 7 minutes has more invested. Their comment is longer,
more specific, and more likely to start a thread.

The same 5 community levers from Law #55 apply — but long-form has additional
tools that Shorts do not have. Use them.

---

## THE 5 COMMUNITY LEVERS (same as Law #55 — required)

Minimum 3 active per long-form video. Same rules as Law #55.

**LEVER 1 — THE OPEN POSITION:** State the take clearly. A neutral take gets no comment.
**LEVER 2 — THE DEBATE SPLIT:** Create two named sides. Both camps need something to say.
**LEVER 3 — THE SPECIFIC DETAIL:** Drop one thing real fans either know or don't know.
**LEVER 4 — THE UNFINISHED THOUGHT:** Closer leaves one thread open. Viewer finishes it.
**LEVER 5 — THE PINNED COMMENT POSITION:** States the creator's position + leaves a specific open thread.

---

## LONG-FORM SPECIFIC ADDITIONS

### THE MID-VIDEO COMMENT TRIGGER
Shorts have one shot at triggering a comment — the closer.
Long-form has multiple shots.

Every 2–3 minutes of long-form should contain at least one line that
could make a viewer pause the video and think "I need to respond to that."

These are mid-video comment triggers. They do not replace the closer.
They stack on top of it.

Types of mid-video comment triggers:
- A claim the viewer disagrees with strongly enough to respond mid-watch
- A specific detail they want to confirm or correct
- A question the creator poses to themselves and does not immediately answer
  (setup for the answer that comes later — but the viewer starts forming their response)

### THE CHAPTER DEBATE
Long-form videos on YouTube can use chapters (timestamps).
Each chapter title is a micro-hook — the viewer reads them before watching.
Chapter titles that contain a stated position generate comments before the video even plays.

Wrong chapter title: "Guts's Backstory"
Right chapter title: "Why Guts Never Actually Gets to Grieve"

The chapter title is a claim. Make every chapter title a claim, not a label.

### THE DESCRIPTION COMMENT SEED
The video description is read by a different viewer than the one watching.
It is often the first thing a subscriber sees in their feed.

The description must contain:
1. The core claim of the video in one sentence (not a summary — the take)
2. One open question that seeds the comment section before anyone watches

Wrong: "In this video I break down why Guts is such a tragic character in Berserk."
Right: "Guts never gets to grieve. Every time he gets close — something takes it from him.
Is that strength or just what's left? Drop your answer below."

### THE REPLY WINDOW
Same rule as Law #55 — reply to every strong comment within the first hour.
Long-form comments are longer and more thoughtful — they deserve a longer reply.
One reply in the first hour signals to the algorithm that the comment section is active.

For long-form specifically:
- Pin the most contested comment (not the most positive — the most argued-about)
- Heart the comments that name a specific detail from the video (signals to the algorithm
  that viewers are watching closely)

---

## COMMUNITY ACTION — IN EVERY LONG-FORM PLANNING SESSION

LONG-FORM COMMUNITY PLAN:
Mid-video triggers: [list the 2–3 lines designed to generate mid-watch responses]
Chapter titles: [confirm each chapter title is a claim, not a label]
Description claim: [one sentence — the take, not the summary]
Description question: [one open question to seed comments]
Pinned comment: [position + open thread — specific to this video]
Reply window: within 1 hour of publishing

---

## AUTO-FAIL CHECK

Before publishing:
[ ] Are at least 3 of the 5 community levers active?
[ ] Are there 2–3 mid-video comment trigger lines?
[ ] Does every chapter title state a claim — not a label?
[ ] Does the description contain the take in one sentence + an open question?
[ ] Is the pinned comment written — not left to post-publish?
[ ] Is the reply window scheduled (within 1 hour of publishing)?

---

# LAW #69 — LONG-FORM FACT VERIFICATION
**System:** AnimeWithSebastian — v5.2
**Added:** June 2026
**Status:** ACTIVE
**Source:** Law #58 (Shorts Pre-Send Verification) adapted for long-form
**Applies to:** All long-form videos (5–8 minutes)

---

## THE LAW

Long-form contains more claims than a Short.
More claims = more chances for one wrong fact to collapse the entire video.

Every specific claim in a long-form video must be verified via live source
before the video is scripted, filmed, or published.

Law #52: NEVER source factual claims from memory. This applies with more
force in long-form — a wrong fact in a 7-minute video sits on screen longer,
reaches more of the audience, and generates more correction comments than a
wrong fact in a 45-second Short.

---

## CLAIM TYPES (same as Law #58, extended for long-form volume)

### TYPE A — GENERAL CHARACTER/SHOW FACT
Example: "Guts uses a sword called the Dragonslayer."
Verification: 1 live search minimum.
These are everywhere in long-form. Do not skip because they seem obvious.
Memory-confirmed ≠ verified.

### TYPE B — CORE CLAIM (COLLAPSE TEST)
The central argument of the video. If this is wrong, the whole video falls apart.
Example: "Berserk's Eclipse arc was the turning point that defined Guts's entire trajectory."
Verification: 2 live searches minimum. Cross-referenced.
Every long-form has exactly one core claim. Find it. Verify it twice.

### TYPE C — YEAR/TIMELINE/NUMBER CLAIM
Example: "Berserk the manga started in 1989."
Verification: 1 live search minimum. These fail silently — a wrong year
in a Short gets 3 comments. A wrong year in a long-form with 1,000 views
gets 40 comments and tanks the credibility of everything else said.

### TYPE D — REAL-WORLD CONNECTION CLAIM
Example: "Kentaro Miura based the Band of the Hawk on medieval European mercenary companies."
Verification: 2 live searches minimum. These are the highest-risk claim type
in long-form because they are the most impressive and the hardest to verify.
If verification fails after 2 searches → REMOVE THE CLAIM. Do not guess.

### TYPE E — COMPARISON CLAIM (long-form only)
Example: "Guts's trauma response is more detailed than any other anime protagonist's."
These appear frequently in long-form arguments and almost never in Shorts.
Verification: Search specifically for counterexamples before making this claim.
If a clear counterexample exists → qualify or remove the claim.
"More detailed than most" is defensible. "More detailed than any" is not.

---

## LONG-FORM FACT BLOCK FORMAT

Every long-form script must include a fact verification block before filming.
Format:

LONG-FORM FACT VERIFICATION BLOCK
Video: [Title]
Core claim (Type B): [claim] — Source: [URL] — Cross-check: [URL]

Supporting claims:
[Claim 1] — Type [A/C/D/E] — Source: [URL]
[Claim 2] — Type [A/C/D/E] — Source: [URL]
[Claim 3] — Type [A/C/D/E] — Source: [URL]
[...]

Any BLOCKED claim (unverified after 2 searches): REMOVED. Replaced with: [replacement or cut]

OVERALL STATUS: PASS — all claims verified
OR: HARD STOP — [claim] blocked, video cannot publish until resolved

---

## THE CORRECTION COMMENT RULE

Long-form videos are watched by more invested fans than Shorts.
Invested fans are more likely to know when a fact is wrong.
A correction comment on a long-form video is more damaging than on a Short
because it sits under a video people are already spending 7 minutes with.

Every correction comment is a failed Law #69 check.
If a long-form video generates a correction comment → identify which claim
failed verification and why. Update the law with the failure case.

---

## AUTO-FAIL CHECK

Before filming or publishing:
[ ] Is the core claim (Type B) verified with 2 live sources?
[ ] Are all Type C (year/number) claims verified?
[ ] Are all Type D (real-world connection) claims verified with 2 sources?
[ ] Are Type E (comparison) claims qualified — not absolute?
[ ] Is the full fact verification block written and attached to the script?
[ ] Are there zero unverified claims remaining?

If any item is NO → do not film. Resolve first.

# LAW #70 — LONG-FORM DANGLING CLAIMS

**Status:** ACTIVE  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Channel:** AnimeWithSebastian (@animewithsebastian)  
**Applies To:** Long-form videos ONLY  
**Short-Form Equivalent:** Law #60 (Dangling Claims)  
**Date Added:** 2026-06-12

---

## CORE RULE

Every claim you open in a long-form video must be closed before the video ends.

A "dangling claim" is any statement that implies a payoff the video never delivers.  
In Shorts, this kills the video in 10 seconds.  
In long-form, it kills watch time and tanks the end-screen retention — the one metric that determines if YouTube recommends the next video.

---

## WHAT A DANGLING CLAIM LOOKS LIKE

**Opening a claim:**
> "There's a reason Sasuke's design was built off Hiei — and it changes how you see the whole rivalry."

**The payoff must come before the outro.**  
If that line lives in Section 1 (hook or setup) and the video ends without addressing it — that is a dangling claim.

**Dangling = the viewer sat through 8 minutes waiting for something you never delivered.**  
They won't come back.

---

## HOW LONG-FORM CHANGES THE RULE

In Law #60, the rule is tight: open a claim → close it within the same Short.  
In long-form, the gap between open and close can span multiple sections.

**That is allowed — with conditions:**

| Condition | Rule |
|---|---|
| Claim opened in Hook | Must be closed no later than Section 4 (Open Closer) |
| Claim opened in Setup | Must be closed no later than Section 4 |
| Claim opened in Argument | Must be closed within the same section or Section 4 |
| Claim opened in Open Closer | Must be closed in the same section — cannot be left open for "Part 2" |
| "Part 2" setups | Only allowed if Section 4 still delivers a complete thought — the viewer must feel like they got something, not got strung along |

**The rule:** The viewer must feel the loop close before the CTA.  
They can want more — but they should not feel cheated.

---

## THE 5-SECTION STRUCTURE (FROM LAW #66)

```
SECTION 1: Hook         — claim may open here
SECTION 2: Setup        — claim may open or continue here
SECTION 3: Argument     — claim develops here
SECTION 4: Open Closer  — ALL open claims must resolve here
SECTION 5: CTA         — no new claims, no new loops
```

The CTA (Section 5) is not a delivery section.  
If a claim reaches Section 5 unresolved, the law is broken.

---

## MULTI-CLAIM TRACKING (ENFORCEMENT)

Long-form videos often open more than one claim. This law covers all of them.

**Before filming or scripting, list every claim the video opens:**

```
CLAIM LOG — [Video Title]
1. [Claim text] → Closes in Section ___
2. [Claim text] → Closes in Section ___
3. [Claim text] → Closes in Section ___
```

If any claim has no assigned close section — cut it or restructure before recording.

---

## WHAT COUNTS AS CLOSING A CLAIM

A claim is closed when:
- The answer, payoff, or verdict is delivered clearly
- The viewer does not have to infer the resolution — it is stated
- The section moves forward after the close, not before it

A claim is NOT closed by:
- Vague gestures ("you can see how this plays out")
- Pivoting to a new topic without addressing the open loop
- Saying "we'll get into that more next time" without giving something now

---

## PART 2 SETUPS — ALLOWED USE

"Part 2" hooks at the end of Section 4 or in the CTA are allowed **only if:**

1. The main argument of the current video is complete
2. The "Part 2" tease introduces a NEW angle — not the unfinished angle from this video
3. The viewer could stop after this video and feel satisfied

**Banned:**
> "But we'll get into why Sasuke actually lost next time."

If that was the whole point of the video and it's never addressed — that is a dangling claim disguised as a Part 2 hook.

**Allowed:**
> "That's the short version — if you want the full breakdown of every fight where that dynamic shows up, that's Part 2."

The current video delivered. Part 2 is an expansion, not a rescue.

---

## ENFORCEMENT — PRE-PUBLISH CHECK

This law is one of six gates in Law #72 (Long-Form Pre-Publish Checklist).

Before any long-form publishes, confirm:
- [ ] Every claim opened is listed in the Claim Log
- [ ] Every claim in the Claim Log has an assigned close section
- [ ] No claim reaches Section 5 (CTA) unresolved
- [ ] If a Part 2 hook exists — the current video still delivers a complete thought

If any box is unchecked — do not publish. Restructure first.

---

## WHY THIS EXISTS

Long-form retention graphs have a specific failure pattern: a sharp drop at the exact timestamp where the viewer realizes the payoff they came for isn't coming.

That drop tells YouTube the video underdelivered.  
Underdelivering kills recommendations.  
Killing recommendations kills the channel's growth path.

Law #66 exists to get AnimeWithSebastian to watch hours.  
Law #70 exists to make sure every minute of those watch hours is earned.

---

# LAW #71 — LONG-FORM STRUCTURE LAW

**Status:** ACTIVE  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Channel:** AnimeWithSebastian (@animewithsebastian)  
**Applies To:** Long-form videos ONLY  
**Short-Form Equivalent:** Law #59 (VO Length / No Essay Rule)  
**Date Added:** 2026-06-12

---

## CORE RULE

Long-form videos have more room than Shorts — that room does not mean academic structure is now allowed.

Long-form IS longer. Long-form IS structured.  
Long-form is NOT an essay. It is NOT a lecture. It is NOT a YouTube video disguised as a thesis paper.

The 5-section structure from Law #66 exists for this reason.  
Every long-form video follows it. No exceptions.

---

## THE BANNED STRUCTURE (ESSAY MODE)

**This framing is permanently banned in long-form:**

```
INTRO:   "Today we're going to talk about X and here's why it matters."
BODY 1:  "First, let's look at..."
BODY 2:  "Second, we can see that..."
BODY 3:  "Third, if you consider..."
CONCLUSION: "So in conclusion, X is Y because of A, B, and C."
```

This is a five-paragraph essay. It reads like a term paper.  
It sounds like a teacher. It doesn't sound like a person who watches anime.

Law #63 bans lecturing.  
Law #71 bans the structure that forces lecturing to happen.

---

## THE REQUIRED STRUCTURE (LAW #66 — 5 SECTIONS)

```
SECTION 1: HOOK          (15–30 sec)
SECTION 2: SETUP         (60–90 sec)
SECTION 3: ARGUMENT      (3–5 min)
SECTION 4: OPEN CLOSER   (60–90 sec)
SECTION 5: CTA           (15–20 sec)
```

**What each section actually sounds like:**

### Section 1 — Hook
Not: "In today's video, we'll be exploring..."  
Yes: Drop the claim. Drop the heat. Make them stay.

The hook is a conversation starter, not a table of contents.  
It opens a loop. It does not explain what the video is about.

### Section 2 — Setup
Not: "First, let's establish the context of..."  
Yes: Ground the argument in something the viewer already knows and cares about.

Setup is the "you already know this but here's why it matters today" section.  
It shortens the distance between where the viewer is and where the argument is going.

### Section 3 — Argument
Not: "Point one. Point two. Point three."  
Yes: One idea, developed — with turns, contradictions, or comparisons that feel like conversation.

The argument section can have multiple moves inside it.  
It cannot feel like a numbered list being read aloud.

Each move should feel like a natural transition in conversation:
> "...and that's the part most people miss — because they're looking at X when the real answer is Y."

Not:
> "Moving on to our second point..."

### Section 4 — Open Closer
Not: "In conclusion, we've learned that..."  
Yes: Land the idea. But leave something slightly open — a question, a comparison, a "you decide."

The Open Closer is not a conclusion. It is the end of the argument with room for the viewer to have a reaction.  
That reaction is what drives comments (Law #68).

### Section 5 — CTA
Not: "Make sure to follow and subscribe for more content!"  
Yes: Casual. Direct. Sounds like something a real person says.

Law #54 (voice) and Law #67 (long-form voice) apply here.  
CTA must match the tone of the rest of the video — not drop into corporate promo mode.

---

## THE TRANSITION RULE

How you move between sections determines whether the video feels like a conversation or a presentation.

**Banned transitions:**
- "Moving on..."
- "Now let's look at..."
- "Next up..."
- "As we can see..."
- "To summarize..."
- "In conclusion..."
- "Now that we've covered X, let's talk about Y..."

**These exist in essay structure. They do not exist in conversation.**

A conversation transitions by following the thought — not by announcing the next thought is coming.

**Allowed transition approach:**
End one idea with a line that naturally leads into the next.

> "...so Hiei never needed to say he cared. His whole fighting style was the proof. And that's exactly why Sasuke's designers borrowed from him — they needed a character who communicated through action, not dialogue."

That line closes one beat and opens the next without announcing the shift.

---

## PACING RULE

Long-form does not mean slow.  
The difference between a 7-minute long-form and a bloated 7-minute ramble is pacing.

**Pacing check per section:**

| Section | Red Flag |
|---|---|
| Hook | Longer than 30 seconds before the claim lands |
| Setup | More than 90 seconds of background before the argument starts |
| Argument | Same idea repeated more than twice in a row — no new angle added |
| Open Closer | Starting to wrap up, then opening a new angle |
| CTA | More than 20 seconds — gets padded and awkward |

If a section is failing its pacing check — cut it down before publishing.

---

## WHAT LONG-FORM ALLOWS THAT SHORTS DO NOT

Long-form unlocks:
- Deeper comparisons (not just a one-line claim — a developed case)
- Multiple examples for the same argument
- A longer setup before the payoff
- More than one "turn" in the argument (introducing a contradiction, then resolving it)
- A real closer that earns the comment section

Long-form does NOT unlock:
- Academic framing
- Thesis/evidence/conclusion structure
- Numbered points read like a list
- Lecturing (Law #63 still applies)
- Padding to hit a runtime (Law #66 — publish when the idea is done)

---

## ENFORCEMENT — PRE-PUBLISH CHECK

This law is one of six gates in Law #72 (Long-Form Pre-Publish Checklist).

Before any long-form publishes, confirm:
- [ ] Video follows the 5-section structure (Law #66)
- [ ] No section uses essay/academic framing
- [ ] No banned transitions appear in the script
- [ ] Argument section develops one idea — not lists points
- [ ] Pacing check passed for all 5 sections
- [ ] CTA sounds like the rest of the video — not a promo drop

If any box is unchecked — revise before publishing.

---

## WHY THIS EXISTS

The reason most anime commentary long-forms fail is not that they're too long.  
It's that they sound like homework.

The viewer came to hear someone talk about anime the way they'd talk about it with a friend.  
The second the video starts sounding like a presentation — they leave.

Law #59 killed essay structure in Shorts.  
Law #71 kills it in long-form before it has a chance to take root.

The 5-section structure from Law #66 is the alternative.  
This law governs how to use it.

# LAW #72 — LONG-FORM PRE-PUBLISH CHECKLIST

**Status:** ACTIVE  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Channel:** AnimeWithSebastian (@animewithsebastian)  
**Applies To:** Long-form videos ONLY  
**Short-Form Equivalent:** Law #58 (Pre-Send Verification — the enforcement gate for Shorts)  
**Date Added:** 2026-06-12

---

## CORE RULE

No long-form video publishes without passing all six gates.

This checklist is not optional. It is not a suggestion.  
It runs before every long-form — the same way Law #58 runs before every Short gets sent.

If any gate fails — the video does not publish until it is fixed.

---

## THE SIX GATES

---

### GATE 1 — VOICE CHECK (Law #67)

**Question:** Does this video sound like a real person talking about anime — or does it sound like a script being performed?

**Pass criteria:**
- [ ] No formal transitions ("Moving on," "As we can see," "In conclusion")
- [ ] No robotic phrasing or AI-sounding delivery patterns
- [ ] The first 30 seconds sounds like the middle of a conversation, not an intro
- [ ] The closing section sounds like a person landing an idea — not wrapping up a report
- [ ] CTA is casual and direct — does not sound like a promo

**If failed:** Rewrite the flagged sections before recording. Do not record over a script that fails the voice check.

---

### GATE 2 — COMMENT ENGINEERING CHECK (Law #68)

**Question:** Does this video give the audience something specific to react to — a take, a comparison, a question they actually want to answer?

**Pass criteria:**
- [ ] At least one moment in Section 3 (Argument) or Section 4 (Open Closer) is designed to generate a response
- [ ] The comment prompt is NOT a generic "What do you think?" — it is tied to the specific argument of the video
- [ ] The closing question or statement is delivered naturally — it does not feel like a planted prompt
- [ ] The video does not end flat — there is a hook for the comment section

**If failed:** Add or rework the Open Closer (Section 4). The comment hook lives there.

---

### GATE 3 — FACT VERIFICATION CHECK (Law #69)

**Question:** Is every specific claim in this video verified against a live source — not memory, not assumption?

**Pass criteria:**
- [ ] Every character stat, power system rule, and timeline claim is sourced to manga, anime episode, or verified wiki
- [ ] Every creator comparison (view counts, upload frequency, format patterns) is sourced to live data from the actual channel
- [ ] Every claim that uses specific numbers ("X times stronger," "Y seconds," "Z episodes") has a source confirmed before recording
- [ ] No claim is included because "it sounds right" — if the source cannot be found, the claim is cut

**Source log (fill before recording):**

```
FACT LOG — [Video Title]
Claim: [exact claim text]
Source: [URL or episode reference]
Verified: YES / NO

Claim: [exact claim text]
Source: [URL or episode reference]
Verified: YES / NO
```

**If failed:** Do not record. Verify the facts or cut the claims. Unverified claims are not published under any circumstances.

---

### GATE 4 — DANGLING CLAIMS CHECK (Law #70)

**Question:** Does every claim opened in this video get resolved before the CTA?

**Pass criteria:**
- [ ] All open claims are listed in the Claim Log
- [ ] Every claim in the Claim Log has an assigned close section (1–4)
- [ ] No claim reaches Section 5 (CTA) unresolved
- [ ] If a Part 2 hook exists — the current video still delivers a complete, satisfying thought

**Claim log (fill before recording):**

```
CLAIM LOG — [Video Title]
1. [Claim text] → Closes in Section ___
2. [Claim text] → Closes in Section ___
3. [Claim text] → Closes in Section ___
```

**If failed:** Restructure the script. Close every loop before Section 5 or cut the unresolvable claim entirely.

### GATE 5 — STRUCTURE CHECK (Law #71)

**Question:** Does this video follow the 5-section structure without falling into essay or lecture mode?

**Pass criteria:**
- [ ] Video uses the 5-section structure: Hook → Setup → Argument → Open Closer → CTA
- [ ] No section uses academic/essay framing (thesis, evidence, conclusion)
- [ ] No banned transitions appear in the script (see Law #71 banned list)
- [ ] Argument section develops one idea — does not list numbered points
- [ ] Pacing check passed:

| Section | Target Length | Passes? |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 15–30 sec | [ ] |
| Setup | 60–90 sec | [ ] |
| Argument | 3–5 min | [ ] |
| Open Closer | 60–90 sec | [ ] |
| CTA | 15–20 sec | [ ] |

**If failed:** Revise the section that failed pacing or structure before recording.

---

### GATE 6 — ORIGINALITY CHECK (Law #65 applied to long-form)

**Question:** Is this video original — a genuine take from AnimeWithSebastian — or is it recreating something that already exists?

**Pass criteria:**
- [ ] If competitor content was reviewed as research, it was used as SIGNAL ONLY — the angle, framing, and voice are original
- [ ] The video does not retell a story that a viewer could find on any other channel — it adds a specific take or angle
- [ ] The channel name and content are independent — no assumption that the viewer knows the channel's branding (Law #64 applied to long-form)
- [ ] The video does not AI-voice the VO — the creator's voice is used (Law #67)

**If failed:** The video is not ready. Rework the angle before recording.

## WHEN THIS CHECKLIST RUNS

- Before every long-form script is finalized
- Before every long-form is recorded
- Before every long-form is uploaded

It is not an afterthought. It is not run after the video is done.  
The earlier a gate fails, the less work is wasted.

Run Gate 3 (Fact Verification) and Gate 4 (Dangling Claims) at the script stage.  
Run Gate 1 (Voice), Gate 5 (Structure), and Gate 6 (Originality) before recording.  
Run Gate 2 (Comment Engineering) before uploading — confirm the Open Closer still works after editing.

---

## WHY THIS EXISTS

Law #58 exists because Shorts were going out with unverified claims.  
Law #72 exists because long-form has more surface area for the same problems — and the consequences are larger.

A Short with a bad claim is 60 seconds of damage.  
A long-form with a bad claim is 8 minutes of damage — and it lives on the channel permanently.

The checklist is the last line of defense.  
It is not optional.
